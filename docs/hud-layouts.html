Layout options

One dataset, one tokyo-night palette, four arrangements · shown at roughly the 190 columns of your terminal

Option 1 · three rows, two columnswidest, shortest

Splits the screen left and right: the left is "who am I working with", the right is "environment and state". Trades width for height and takes only three rows.

Gains
  • Three rows only, leaving the most room for input
  • The right column is fixed, so the eye knows where to look
Costs
  • The right column is sacrificed first as the terminal narrows
  • Dense; it takes some getting used to

Option 2 · five rows, loose alignmentmost readable

Labels are left-aligned into a column, bars grow to 24 cells, and separators are three spaces rather than crammed together. The eye scans down one vertical line.

  • Aligned labels, the cheapest to scan
  • Adding Env / Tools labels removes the guesswork about what a row is
  • Long bars make percentage changes visible
Costs

Option 3 · four rows, zonedcompromise

The meters take a full-width row of their own, while environment and state share the two ends of another. repo sits at the right of the first row.

Option 4 · two rows, very widesmallest footprint

Everything in two rows. Suits running pi in a Herdr pane where vertical space is tight.

Gains
  • Two rows only, a lifesaver in a narrow pane
Costs
  • Labels shrink to Ctx/Ses/Cch, which reads worse
  • Very width-dependent; it collapses as soon as it narrows
Whichever you pick, which rows appear is still decided by lines in the config, and you can add or remove them any time with /pi-statusline-hud. The options differ in default grouping and spacing — the density key, switchable later.
